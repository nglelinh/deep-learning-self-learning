Words are discrete symbols: as raw integer IDs, `"cat"` (ID 412), `"dog"` (ID 9817), and `"carburetor"` (ID 413) are completely equidistant, giving a neural network zero geometric clue that cats and dogs share meaning. Natural Language Processing took off the moment researchers turned discrete tokens into dense continuous vectors—first static embeddings like Word2Vec and GloVe, then contextual Transformer representations like BERT and GPT—turning syntax and semantics into linear algebra.

## Background Knowledge Map: The Trajectory of Deep NLP

- **Pillar 1 — Distributional Semantics & Vector Geometry**: Operationalizes J. R. Firth's dictum (*"You shall know a word by the company it keeps"*) by encoding co-occurrence patterns into dense vectors where cosine similarity tracks semantic relatedness.
- **Pillar 2 — Subword Tokenization (BPE / WordPiece / Unigram)**: Splits rare and morphological words into reusable subword pieces so vocabularies stay compact ($\sim 32\text{k}$–$128\text{k}$) with zero out-of-vocabulary `<UNK>` failures.
- **Pillar 3 — Static vs. Contextual Representations**: Contrasts one-vector-per-word-type models (where *"river bank"* and *"bank account"* share one vector) with Transformer contextual states that dynamically disambiguate every token from its surrounding sentence.
- **Pillar 4 — Transfer from Pretrained Language Models**: Connects masked and autoregressive language modeling directly to downstream classification, NER, QA, translation, and modern LLM prompting.

### Step-by-Step Worked Example (Read Slowly): Why One-Hot Vectors Fail Where Dense Embeddings Succeed

Consider a 4-word vocabulary `{"cat": 0, "dog": 1, "kitten": 2, "truck": 3}`.

1. **One-hot representation**: $e_{\text{cat}} = [1,0,0,0]$, $e_{\text{dog}} = [0,1,0,0]$, $e_{\text{truck}} = [0,0,0,1]$.
   $$\cos(e_{\text{cat}}, e_{\text{dog}}) = 0, \qquad \cos(e_{\text{cat}}, e_{\text{truck}}) = 0.$$
   Every distinct pair is strictly orthogonal ($90^\circ$ apart)!
2. **Dense 2D learned embeddings** (axis 1: animacy, axis 2: domestic/small):
   $$v_{\text{cat}} = [0.9, 0.8], \quad v_{\text{dog}} = [0.9, 0.7], \quad v_{\text{truck}} = [-0.8, -0.6].$$
   - Norms: $\|v_{\text{cat}}\| = \sqrt{0.81 + 0.64} = 1.204$, $\|v_{\text{dog}}\| = \sqrt{0.81 + 0.49} = 1.140$, $\|v_{\text{truck}}\| = 1.000$.
   - $\cos(v_{\text{cat}}, v_{\text{dog}}) = \frac{0.81 + 0.56}{1.204 \times 1.140} = \frac{1.37}{1.373} \approx +0.998$ (nearly parallel!).
   - $\cos(v_{\text{cat}}, v_{\text{truck}}) = \frac{-0.72 - 0.48}{1.204 \times 1.000} = -0.997$ (nearly opposite!).
3. **Takeaway**: Dense embeddings compress a $50{,}000$-dimensional sparse one-hot space into a few hundred continuous dimensions where semantic similarity becomes a single dot product.
