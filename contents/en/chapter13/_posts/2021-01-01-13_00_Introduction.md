A vanilla autoencoder is like a meticulous librarian who files every book wherever there happens to be shelf space—great for finding books you already own, terrible if you reach blindly onto a shelf expecting a coherent new novel. In 2013, Kingma & Welling and Rezende et al. solved this by turning the encoder into a *probabilistic* map: instead of pinning each image to a single rigid point $z$, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) maps it to a soft Gaussian cloud pulled gently toward $\mathcal{N}(0, I)$, eliminating dead zones so every random draw from the prior decodes into plausible data.

## Key ideas: Foundations of Variational Autoencoders

- **Pillar 1 — Variational Inference & the ELBO**: Replaces the intractable marginal log-likelihood $\log p_\theta(x)$ with a tractable Evidence Lower Bound balancing reconstruction accuracy against prior regularization.
- **Pillar 2 — Amortized Inference**: Uses a single neural network $q_\phi(z \mid x)$ to predict posterior parameters $(\mu_\phi(x), \log\sigma^2_\phi(x))$ for any input $x$ in one forward pass, avoiding per-datapoint optimization.
- **Pillar 3 — The Reparameterization Trick**: Separates stochastic sampling $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ from differentiable parameters via $z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$, allowing exact low-variance backpropagation through random nodes.
- **Pillar 4 — Latent Space Continuity & Generation**: Guarantees smooth semantic interpolation and direct ancestral sampling $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \to x \sim p_\theta(x \mid z)$.

### Worked example: One Forward Pass of a 1D VAE Posterior

Suppose for an input $x$, our encoder outputs $\mu = 1.0$ and $\log\sigma^2 = 0.0$ (so $\sigma^2 = e^0 = 1.0$, $\sigma = 1.0$), and we draw $\epsilon = -0.5$ from $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$.

1. **Reparameterized latent sample**:
   $$z = \mu + \sigma \cdot \epsilon = 1.0 + 1.0 \cdot (-0.5) = 0.5.$$
2. **Closed-form KL divergence against standard normal prior $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$**:
   $$D_{\text{KL}}\big(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\big) = -\frac{1}{2}\big(1 + \log\sigma^2 - \mu^2 - \sigma^2\big) = -\frac{1}{2}(1 + 0 - 1^2 - 1) = 0.5\text{ nats}.$$
3. **Gradient of $z$ with respect to encoder parameters**:
   $$\frac{\partial z}{\partial \mu} = 1, \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial \sigma} = \epsilon = -0.5.$$
4. **Takeaway**: Both the KL penalty ($0.5$ nats pulling $\mu$ toward $0$) and the exact pathwise gradients ($\partial z/\partial \mu = 1$, $\partial z/\partial \sigma = -0.5$) are completely analytical and differentiable.

Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are probabilistic generative models that learn structured latent representations. By combining variational inference with neural networks, VAEs can generate new samples and interpolate smoothly in latent space. This chapter covers the VAE architecture, ELBO objective, reparameterization trick, and applications.
