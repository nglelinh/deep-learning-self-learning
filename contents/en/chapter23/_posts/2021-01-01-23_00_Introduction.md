A state-of-the-art model that needs four $30,000 GPUs and 800 milliseconds per inference is a research demo, not a product. Efficient Deep Learning asks a sharper engineering question: how much of a neural network's memory footprint, FLOPs, and energy is genuinely essential, and how much can be stripped away via **pruning**, **quantization**, **knowledge distillation**, and **hardware-aware architectures** without surrendering accuracy?

## Key ideas: The Four Levers of Efficient Deep Learning

- **Pillar 1 — Pruning (Unstructured vs. Structured)**: Removes low-magnitude weights (high theoretical sparsity) or entire channels/heads (real wall-clock GPU speedup without specialized sparse kernels).
- **Pillar 2 — Quantization (FP32 → FP16/BF16 → INT8/INT4)**: Maps continuous weights and activations onto low-bit integer grids, slashing VRAM footprint and memory-bandwidth bottlenecks by $2\times$–$8\times$.
- **Pillar 3 — Knowledge Distillation**: Trains a compact student model to mimic a large teacher's softened probability distributions ("dark knowledge"), preserving inter-class similarity structure.
- **Pillar 4 — Hardware-Aware Architectures & Roofline Thinking**: Uses depthwise separable convolutions (MobileNet) and compound scaling (EfficientNet), optimizing for *memory bandwidth* (bytes moved) as much as raw FLOPs.

### Worked example: Memory & Bandwidth Reduction from FP32 to INT4

Consider a 7-billion-parameter ($7 \times 10^9$) dense neural network deployed on a GPU with $320\text{ GB/s}$ memory bandwidth.

1. **Weight storage footprint**:
   - FP32 ($4\text{ bytes/param}$): $7 \times 10^9 \times 4 = 28.0\text{ GB}$ (won't even fit on a 24 GB GPU!).
   - FP16/BF16 ($2\text{ bytes/param}$): $14.0\text{ GB}$.
   - INT8 ($1\text{ byte/param}$): $7.0\text{ GB}$.
   - INT4 ($0.5\text{ bytes/param}$): $3.5\text{ GB}$ ($8\times$ smaller than FP32!).
2. **Memory-bound single-token decode latency lower bound** (reading all weights once per token):
   - FP16: $14.0\text{ GB} / 320\text{ GB/s} = 43.75\text{ ms/token}$ ($\approx 22.9\text{ tok/s}$).
   - INT4: $3.5\text{ GB} / 320\text{ GB/s} = 10.94\text{ ms/token}$ ($\approx 91.4\text{ tok/s}$ — a $4\times$ speedup!).
3. **Takeaway**: Because autoregressive decoding is memory-bandwidth bound, shrinking weight bit-width delivers an almost linear speedup in tokens per second, not just disk savings.

Efficient deep learning makes models faster, smaller, and more deployable. This chapter covers model compression (pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation), efficient architectures (MobileNet, EfficientNet), hardware acceleration (GPUs, TPUs), mixed-precision training, and deployment strategies for edge devices and production systems.
