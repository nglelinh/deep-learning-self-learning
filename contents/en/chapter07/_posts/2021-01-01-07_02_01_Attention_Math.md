# Attention Mathematics

![Attention Mechanism Visualization](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tensorflow/nmt/master/nmt/g3doc/img/attention_mechanism.jpg)
*Classic neural machine translation attention: decoder states form queries over encoder keys/values. Source: TensorFlow NMT tutorial*

This lesson develops attention **as a precise mathematical object**. Fundamentals (07-01) already introduced the story; here we unify notation, compare score families rigorously, vectorize everything for batches, and set up multi-head attention and masking as first-class equations—not afterthoughts.

---

## 1. Concept Overview

At the highest level, attention is a **parameterized, differentiable soft lookup**. Given a query vector that encodes “what do I need now?”, attention compares that query to a set of keys, converts similarities into a probability distribution, and returns the corresponding mixture of values.

Three design axes completely determine a concrete mechanism:

1. **Where Q, K, V come from** — decoder vs encoder (cross-attention), or the same sequence (self-attention).  
2. **How similarity is scored** — additive, bilinear, or scaled dot product.  
3. **What positions are allowed** — full attention, padding masks, causal masks, or sparse patterns.

Everything famous in the literature (Bahdanau NMT attention, Luong variants, Transformer self-attention, multi-head attention) is a point in this three-dimensional design space.

A useful algebraic slogan:

$$
\text{Attention}(\mathbf{q}, \{(\mathbf{k}_j,\mathbf{v}_j)\}_j)
= \sum_j \alpha_j \mathbf{v}_j,
\quad
\alpha = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathrm{score}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}_\cdot)).
$$

Matrix forms replace the sum with a product; multi-head attention stacks several independent copies of this map.

![Step 1: $$Q K^\top$$](/deep-learning-self-learning/img/chapter_img/chapter07/attn_qk_matmul.jpg)
*Figure: Raw scores = product of $$Q$$ with $$K^\top$$ (one query row per token). (Illustration from a Transformer Attention video)*

## 2. Mathematical Foundation

### 2.1 Single-query attention

Let

- query $$\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_q}$$,
- keys $$\mathbf{k}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$$, $$j=1,\ldots,L$$,
- values $$\mathbf{v}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{d_v}$$.

**Scores**

$$e_j = \mathrm{score}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{k}_j) \in \mathbb{R}.$$

**Weights**

$$\alpha_j = \frac{\exp(e_j)}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\exp(e_\ell)}.$$

**Output**

$$\mathbf{c} = \sum_{j=1}^{L} \alpha_j \mathbf{v}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{d_v}.$$

Properties that always hold (without masking):

- $$\alpha_j \ge 0$$, $$\sum_j \alpha_j = 1$$,
- $$\mathbf{c}$$ lies in the convex hull of $$\{\mathbf{v}_j\}$$,
- if one score dominates, $$\mathbf{c}$$ approaches that value (soft argmax).

### 2.2 Score families

**Additive (Bahdanau)**

$$\mathrm{score}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{v}_a^\top \tanh(\mathbf{W}_q\mathbf{q} + \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{k}).$$

- Flexible: query and key can live in different raw spaces before projection.  
- Cost: extra nonlinearity and vector $$\mathbf{v}_a$$; less GPU-friendly than pure matmuls when $$L$$ is large.

**Bilinear / general**

$$\mathrm{score}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{W} \mathbf{k}.$$

Learns a metric. Reduces to a dot product when $$\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}$$.

**Dot product**

$$\mathrm{score}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{k}.$$

Fast and natural when $$\mathbf{q}$$ and $$\mathbf{k}$$ already share a space (typical after learned projections).

**Scaled dot product (Transformer)**

$$\mathrm{score}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}) = \frac{\mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{d_k}}.$$

### 2.3 Why scaling matters (variance argument)

Suppose coordinates of $$\mathbf{q}$$ and $$\mathbf{k}$$ are i.i.d. with mean 0 and variance 1. Then

$$\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}) = d_k.$$

For large $$d_k$$, $$|\mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}|$$ tends to be large. Softmax of large-magnitude logits is nearly one-hot:

$$\alpha \approx \mathbf{e}_{j^\star}, \quad j^\star=\arg\max_j e_j,$$

and gradients w.r.t. non-max positions vanish. Dividing by $$\sqrt{d_k}$$ restores variance $$\approx 1$$, independent of dimension, so training can learn *graded* attention, not only hard selection.

### 2.4 Batched matrix form

Pack $$L_q$$ queries and $$L_k$$ keys:

$$
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_q\times d_k},\quad
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_k\times d_k},\quad
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_k\times d_v}.
$$

Then

$$
\mathbf{A} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{L_q\times L_k},
\qquad
\mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{L_q\times d_v}.
$$

With batch and heads:

$$
\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times L\times d_k}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{scores }\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times H\times L_q\times L_k}.
$$

All pairwise similarities become one batched matmul—this is why attention thrives on GPUs.

### 2.5 Encoder–decoder (cross) attention

At decoder step $$t$$ with state $$\mathbf{s}_t$$ and encoder states $$\mathbf{H}=[\mathbf{h}_1;\ldots;\mathbf{h}_{L_x}]\in\mathbb{R}^{L_x\times d}$$:

$$
\mathbf{q}_t = f_q(\mathbf{s}_t),\quad
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}^K,\quad
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}^V,
$$

$$
\mathbf{c}_t = \mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{q}_t,\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}).
$$

![Cross-attention: Q from decoder, K/V from encoder](/deep-learning-self-learning/img/chapter_img/chapter07/attn_cross_qkv_projections.jpg)
*Figure: Queries from (masked) decoder output; keys/values from the encoder. (Illustration from a Transformer Attention video)*

![Full cross-attention pipeline](/deep-learning-self-learning/img/chapter_img/chapter07/attn_cross_attention_pipeline.jpg)
*Figure: Project → $$QK^\top$$ → softmax → × V → cross-attention output. (Illustration from a Transformer Attention video)*

Classic Bahdanau uses additive score between $$\mathbf{s}_t$$ and each $$\mathbf{h}_i$$ without necessarily factoring separate $$\mathbf{W}^K,\mathbf{W}^V$$ the Transformer way—but the *role* decomposition is the same: query from decoder, memory from encoder.

### 2.6 Self-attention

For input matrix $$\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d_{\mathrm{model}}}$$:

$$
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}^Q,\quad
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}^K,\quad
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}^V,
$$

$$
\mathrm{SelfAttn}(\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V}).
$$

Row $$i$$ of the output is a mixture of all value rows, with mixture weights depending on how well query $$i$$ matches every key.

**Path length.** Any position can influence any other in **one** attention layer ($$O(1)$$ sequential steps), versus $$O(L)$$ along an RNN chain. That is the structural reason self-attention captures long-range dependencies more easily.

**Complexity.** Dense self-attention is $$O(L^2 d)$$ time and $$O(L^2)$$ memory for the score matrix—fine for moderate $$L$$, painful for very long documents (hence later sparse/linear attention research).

### 2.7 Multi-head attention

With $$H$$ heads and $$d_k=d_v=d_{\mathrm{model}}/H$$:

$$
\mathrm{head}_h=\mathrm{Attention}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_h^Q,\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_h^K,\mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_h^V),
$$

$$
\mathrm{MultiHead}(\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{Concat}(\mathrm{head}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{head}_H)\,\mathbf{W}^O.
$$

Dimensions:

- $$\mathbf{W}_h^Q,\mathbf{W}_h^K\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_k}$$,
- $$\mathbf{W}_h^V\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}\times d_v}$$,
- $$\mathbf{W}^O\in\mathbb{R}^{H d_v\times d_{\mathrm{model}}}$$.

**Why multiple heads?** Softmax attention is a single soft distribution per query. One distribution often collapses to one mode. Separate heads provide several distributions in parallel—empirically specializing (syntax vs locality vs rare long links)—then $$\mathbf{W}^O$$ mixes them.

### 2.8 Masking as an additive operation on logits

Let $$\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top/\sqrt{d_k}$$. Define an additive mask $$\mathbf{M}$$ of the same broadcastable shape:

$$
\mathbf{M}_{ij}=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if position }j\text{ is allowed for query }i,\\
-\infty & \text{if forbidden.}
\end{cases}
$$

Then

$$
\mathbf{A}=\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{M}).
$$

Because $$\exp(-\infty)=0$$, forbidden keys get zero weight **and** correct gradients (unlike zeroing weights *after* softmax).

**Padding mask.** Forbid keys that are pad tokens (per batch row).  
**Causal mask.** Forbid $$j>i$$ for autoregressive decoding: $$\mathbf{M}=\mathrm{triu}(-\infty)$$ strictly above the diagonal.

### 2.9 Residual form used in practice

In deep stacks one almost always uses

$$
\mathbf{X} \leftarrow \mathbf{X} + \mathrm{Dropout}(\mathrm{MultiHead}(\mathrm{Norm}(\mathbf{X})))
$$

(or post-norm variants). Mathematics of a single attention map does not include residuals, but **trainability** of deep attention models depends on them. Keep this distinction clear: attention defines a layer map; residuals define how maps compose.

## 3. Example / Intuition

### 3.1 Cross-attention with explicit numbers

Encoder states (already projected keys = values for simplicity), $$d=2$$:

$$
\mathbf{h}_1=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}\ (``I"),\quad
\mathbf{h}_2=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}\ (``love"),\quad
\mathbf{h}_3=\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\ (``ML").
$$

Decoder query $$\mathbf{q}=\begin{bmatrix}0.9\\0.2\end{bmatrix}$$. Dot-product scores:

$$
e=(0.9,\ 0.2,\ 1.1).
$$

Softmax (approx.):

$$
\alpha \approx (0.34,\ 0.17,\ 0.49).
$$

Context:

$$
\mathbf{c}\approx 0.34\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}
+0.17\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}
+0.49\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}0.83\\0.66\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Most mass is on “ML,” but “I” still contributes—soft alignment, not hard pointer.

### 3.2 Self-attention as a data-dependent mixing matrix

For self-attention, $$\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times L}$$ is a **row-stochastic** matrix (each row sums to 1). The map

$$\mathbf{X}\mapsto \mathbf{A}\mathbf{V}$$

is a linear mixture of values, but $$\mathbf{A}$$ *depends nonlinearly* on $$\mathbf{X}$$ through QKV projections and softmax. That input dependence is what makes attention strictly more expressive than a fixed convolution kernel of the same support.

### 3.3 Multi-head as a block diagonal story

Imagine two heads with orthogonal interests: head 1 peeks at adjacent tokens; head 2 peeks at a coreferent noun. Concatenation preserves both signals in different feature slices; $$\mathbf{W}^O$$ can then build features that need *both* adjacency and coreference. A single head would have to compromise with one distribution.

## 4. Code Snippet (math-facing)

This snippet is intentionally short: it mirrors the equations. Full demos live in the implementation lessons.

```python
import numpy as np

def attention_math(Q, K, V, mask=None):
    """
    Q: (Lq, dk), K: (Lk, dk), V: (Lk, dv)
    mask: (Lq, Lk) with True = forbid
    """
    dk = Q.shape[-1]
    S = Q @ K.T / np.sqrt(dk)
    if mask is not None:
        S = np.where(mask, -1e9, S)
    S = S - S.max(axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    A = np.exp(S)
    A = A / A.sum(axis=-1, keepdims=True)
    return A @ V, A

# Causal self-attention on random features
L, d = 5, 16
X = np.random.randn(L, d)
Wq, Wk, Wv = [np.random.randn(d, d) * 0.1 for _ in range(3)]
Q, K, V = X @ Wq, X @ Wk, X @ Wv
causal = np.triu(np.ones((L, L), dtype=bool), 1)
Y, A = attention_math(Q, K, V, mask=causal)
assert Y.shape == (L, d)
assert np.allclose(A.sum(-1), 1)
assert np.allclose(np.triu(A, 1), 0, atol=1e-6)  # no future mass
```

## 5. Related Concepts

- **Kernel smoothing / Nadaraya–Watson estimators** — attention is a learned kernel smoother over values.  
- **Content-addressable memory** — keys address content; queries retrieve.  
- **Graph message passing** — self-attention on a complete graph with data-dependent edge weights.  
- **Convolutions** — fixed local kernels vs dynamic global weights.  
- **Sparse attention / linear attention** — approximations when $$O(L^2)$$ is unacceptable.

## 7. Common Pitfalls and Tricks

- **Mixing up $$L_q$$ and $$L_k$$ axes** when implementing masks.  
- **Applying mask after softmax** — wrong gradients and residual leakage.  
- **Omitting scale** at large $$d_k$$.  
- **Assuming $$\mathbf{A}$$ is symmetric** in self-attention — it is not; rows are distributions over keys, not undirected edges.  
- **Trick:** verify causal attention with the test `triu(A,1)≈0`.  
- **Trick:** unit-test multi-head reshape by checking total element count and a tiny hand example.

---

## 8. Key Takeaways

1. Attention is always **score → softmax → weighted values**; only score, sources of QKV, and masks change.  
2. **Scaling** is a variance-control device required for high-dimensional dot products.  
3. **Matrix form** $$\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V$$ is the industrial interface.  
4. **Self-attention** gives $$O(1)$$ path length at $$O(L^2)$$ cost.  
5. **Multi-head** = several soft distributions + output mix.  
6. **Masks modify logits**, not post-hoc weights.
