# LSTM and GRU: Conquering the Vanishing Gradient

![LSTM Architecture](/deep-learning-self-learning/img/chapter_img/chapter06/lstm_architecture.png)
*Hình ảnh: Kiến trúc LSTM với cell state và các gates. Nguồn: Wikimedia Commons*

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks represent one of the most important architectural innovations in the history of neural networks. They solve the **vanishing gradient problem** that fundamentally limited vanilla RNNs, enabling learning of dependencies spanning hundreds or thousands of time steps.

![GRU Architecture](/deep-learning-self-learning/img/chapter_img/chapter06/gru_architecture.png)
*Hình ảnh: Kiến trúc GRU với 2 gates (reset và update). Nguồn: Wikimedia Commons*

---

## The Problem LSTMs Solve

Recall from Chapter 5 that vanilla RNNs suffer from vanishing gradients: when backpropagating through many time steps, gradients shrink exponentially, making it impossible to learn long-range dependencies. Information about the beginning of a sentence is essentially forgotten by the time we reach the end.

**The key insight of LSTMs**: Create an explicit pathway for information to flow unchanged through time, bypassing the repeated nonlinear transformations that cause gradient decay.

---

## The Cell State: A Highway for Information

LSTMs introduce a **cell state** $$\mathbf{c}_t$$ — a separate pathway that runs parallel to the hidden state. The cell state can carry information across many time steps with only minor linear interactions:

$$\mathbf{c}_t = \mathbf{f}_t \odot \mathbf{c}_{t-1} + \mathbf{i}_t \odot \tilde{\mathbf{c}}_t$$

When the forget gate $$\mathbf{f}_t \approx 1$$, information flows through unchanged. This is dramatically different from vanilla RNNs where information must pass through weight matrices and nonlinearities at every step.

---

## Gates: Learnable Information Control

LSTMs use three types of **gates** — sigmoid layers that output values in [0,1] — to control information flow:

| Gate | Function | Equation |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Forget** | What to discard from cell state | $$\mathbf{f}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_f[\mathbf{h}_{t-1}; \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_f)$$ |
| **Input** | What new information to add | $$\mathbf{i}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_i[\mathbf{h}_{t-1}; \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_i)$$ |
| **Output** | What to expose to downstream | $$\mathbf{o}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_o[\mathbf{h}_{t-1}; \mathbf{x}_t] + \mathbf{b}_o)$$ |

Think of gates as learnable switches: the network learns **when to remember**, **when to forget**, and **when to act** on its memory.

---

## GRU: A Simpler Alternative

The Gated Recurrent Unit (2014) simplifies LSTMs while preserving their essential benefits:
- **Two gates** instead of three (reset and update)
- **No separate cell state** — hidden state does everything
- **~25% fewer parameters** — faster training, easier tuning

For many tasks, GRU performs comparably to LSTM. A good heuristic: **start with GRU** (simpler), switch to LSTM only if performance plateaus.

## Historical Impact

LSTMs dominated sequence modeling from the late 1990s through the mid-2010s, enabling breakthroughs in:
- Machine translation (Google Translate, 2016)
- Speech recognition (Siri, Google Voice)
- Handwriting recognition
- Music generation
- Time series forecasting

Even after Transformers emerged as the dominant architecture for NLP, LSTMs remain important:
- **Lower memory cost**: $$O(n)$$ vs Transformers' $$O(n^2)$$
- **Online processing**: Can process sequences naturally as they arrive
- **Long sequence efficiency**: Better for very long sequences with limited compute

Understanding LSTMs deeply — both their elegance and limitations — provides crucial context for appreciating why attention mechanisms and Transformers evolved.
